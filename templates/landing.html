How it works

Three steps, about a minute

1

Drop in a PDF

A chapter, a slide deck, a set of lecture notes. Anything text-based, up to 10 MB. Name it or let the filename do the work.

2

It gets read properly

The text is split along paragraph boundaries so ideas stay intact, then written up as cards — with the card count scaled to how much is actually in the document.

3

Study on schedule

Reveal, grade yourself honestly, move on. Each card comes back at a widening interval — and the ones you fumble come back sooner.

Not a text dump

Four kinds of card, mixed on purpose

Highlighting a paragraph doesn't test anything. Every deck comes out as a blend of question types, because recalling a definition and applying an idea are different skills — and you want both.

Cards are written to stand on their own. No “according to the passage…”, no page-number trivia, no questions that answer themselves.

The schedule

Intervals that stretch as you get it right

{{ app_name }} uses SM-2, the algorithm behind SuperMemo and Anki. Get a card right and the gap widens. Miss it and the card drops back into the short queue, while its ease factor quietly falls so it stays harder for longer.

Features

Everything the studying actually needs

Drag, drop, done

Generation runs in the background, so the upload never hangs. Close the tab — the deck carries on building.

Keyboard-first study

Space reveals, 1–4 grades. A full session without reaching for the mouse.

Honest progress

New, learning, review, mastered — per deck, at a glance. Mastered means 21+ days and four clean recalls, not a participation badge.

Yours to edit

Search the deck, filter by status, rewrite any card that isn't pulling its weight, bin the ones that aren't.

Private by account

Passwords are hashed with bcrypt and sessions are signed. Every deck, card and review belongs to you alone.

Sized to the source

A three-page handout gets a handful of cards, not seventy. The deck scales to how much is genuinely in the document.
